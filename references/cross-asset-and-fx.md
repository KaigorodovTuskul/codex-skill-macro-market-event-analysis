# Cross-asset and FX analysis

## Rates and bonds

Bond prices and yields move inversely. For a small yield change:

`ΔP / P ≈ -modified duration × Δyield + 0.5 × convexity × (Δyield)^2`

Do not convert a yield move into a price move without maturity, coupon, duration, and starting yield.

A useful decomposition is:

`nominal yield ≈ expected real short rates + expected inflation + term premium`

Treat this as an analytical identity with model-dependent components, not directly observed truth. Long yields can fall because expected policy rates, inflation compensation, term premium, duration supply, or liquidity premium changed.

## Dollar indices and bilateral FX

DXY is a weighted basket dominated by developed-market currencies. It does not state what the dollar did against every currency and does not determine a third-currency cross.

Using common quotations:

`RUB per CNY = (RUB per USD) / (CNY per USD)`

Therefore a fall in USD/RUB does not by itself determine CNY/RUB. The result also depends on USD/CNY, Chinese policy, Russian trade flows, sanctions, rates, commodity receipts, capital controls, and local liquidity.

For every FX conclusion state:

- quotation direction;
- nominal or real rate;
- spot, forward, or hedged return;
- relevant rate differential and funding currency;
- intervention, fixing, convertibility, and capital-control regime;
- trade, commodity, reserve, and geopolitical drivers.

## Gold and metals

Gold commonly responds to real yields, the dollar, inflation and tail-risk demand, central-bank purchases, positioning, and physical flows. A lower dollar or lower real yield can support gold, but neither guarantees a rise.

Industrial metals depend additionally on global growth, China, inventories, energy costs, mine supply, treatment charges, and producer currencies. Do not generalize a gold move to all metals.

## Cryptoassets

Cryptoassets can react to dollar liquidity and real yields, but also to leverage, liquidations, ETF and treasury-company flows, protocol news, regulation, stablecoins, token-specific supply, and risk appetite. Treat “digital gold” or “liquidity proxy” as hypotheses to test, not identities.

## Equities and credit

Lower long yields can raise present values and ease refinancing, but the sign depends on why yields fell. A growth scare can lower yields while hurting cyclical equities and credit. Higher term premium can pressure long-duration equities, leveraged firms, real estate, and sovereign borrowers even if policy rates are unchanged.
