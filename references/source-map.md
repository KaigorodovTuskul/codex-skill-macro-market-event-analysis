# Source map

Use primary sources for the event and the narrowest market source for the observed reaction.

## Event and policy sources

- finance ministry, debt-management office, central bank, regulator, legislature, court, or international organization;
- official release, legal text, minutes, transcript, schedule, tender, auction, operation result, and balance sheet;
- fiscal plans, borrowing estimates, debt composition, cash balance, and issuance calendar;
- central-bank reserves, facilities, securities holdings, collateral rules, and policy projections.

Check whether a release announces, authorizes, schedules, executes, or settles an action.

## Market data

- official debt and auction data;
- exchange, benchmark administrator, central bank, and regulated trading-venue data;
- yield curve, inflation compensation, real yields, term-premium estimates, swaps, repo, and cross-currency basis;
- spot, futures, options, volume, open interest, volatility, and market depth;
- fund flows, positioning, custody, reserves, and official international-capital data.

Record ticker or series, quotation convention, timezone, frequency, adjustment, and exact window. A screenshot or current quote does not establish the historical path by itself.

For point-in-time analysis, retain the release calendar and data vintage. Revised macro series, backfilled histories, constituent changes, benchmark methodology changes, futures rolls, corporate actions, and vendor timezone defaults can create hindsight or measurement bias.

## Macro and real-economy data

- inflation, employment, wages, growth, consumption, investment, productivity, trade, and fiscal statistics;
- surveys, nowcasts, expectations, revisions, and surprise indices;
- corporate financing, defaults, bank lending, mortgage, and credit-standard data;
- commodity inventories, physical premiums, freight, production, and trade flows.

## Independent interpretation

- established financial media for chronology and market commentary;
- dealer and asset-manager research with disclosed assumptions;
- academic papers and central-bank research for mechanisms;
- specialist market commentary, social media, and rumors as leads only.

Do not use a commentator's causal explanation as proof. Reconstruct timing and mechanism independently.

## Search patterns

- `[official actor] exact program announcement operation result`
- `[instrument] schedule auction buyback maturity amount`
- `[event date and timezone] yield dollar gold crypto reaction`
- `[policy] balance sheet reserves issuance liquidity mechanism`
- `[currency pair] central bank fixing intervention capital controls`
- `[asset move] simultaneous data release speech auction`
- local-language institution name + official instrument and date

## Source cautions

- headline time can differ from original release time;
- futures and spot markets have different sessions;
- yields are not bond returns;
- DXY is not a universal dollar exchange rate;
- an authorization ceiling is not executed volume;
- a buyback can be financed by new issuance;
- central-bank purchases and treasury debt management have different balance-sheet effects;
- nominal and real yields can move for different reasons;
- correlation after a headline is not sufficient causal identification;
- revised macro data can change the historical interpretation.
- two vendors can repeat the same underlying observation and are not independent confirmation;
- a current database history can contain revisions unavailable to the market at the event time.
