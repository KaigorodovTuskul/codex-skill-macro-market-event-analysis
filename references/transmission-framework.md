# Transmission framework

## Identify the balance-sheet change

Start with sectoral balance sheets rather than asset-price storytelling:

- Which entity buys, sells, issues, retires, guarantees, taxes, spends, or changes collateral rules?
- What asset and liability move on each balance sheet?
- Is liquidity created, destroyed, transferred, or merely redistributed?
- Is gross sovereign debt reduced, its maturity composition changed, or one issue exchanged for another?
- Does bank-reserve supply change, or only the market composition of securities?

This separates central-bank asset purchases from finance-ministry debt management, and fiscal stimulus from refinancing operations.

## Shock taxonomy

### Monetary-policy shock

Policy rates, forward guidance, reserve supply, asset purchases, quantitative tightening, collateral policy, or standing facilities.

Primary variables: expected policy path, real rates, curve shape, reserve scarcity, credit conditions, currency.

### Fiscal and sovereign-financing shock

Deficit, taxes, spending, issuance size, maturity mix, buybacks, cash balance, debt ceiling, or demand at auctions.

Primary variables: net duration supply, term premium, Treasury cash, funding needs, crowding out, currency confidence.

### Market-liquidity and plumbing shock

Dealer balance-sheet capacity, off-the-run liquidity, repo, collateral scarcity, margin, basis trades, volatility control, or forced deleveraging.

Primary variables: bid-ask spreads, repo rates, basis, depth, volatility, liquidation pressure.

### Macro-data shock

Inflation, employment, growth, productivity, wages, consumption, or external balances relative to expectations.

Primary variables: expected policy path, earnings, real income, default risk, currency.

### Regulatory, geopolitical, and supply shock

Sanctions, tariffs, capital controls, conflict, commodity disruption, technology policy, or financial regulation.

Primary variables: scarcity, risk premium, trade and payment routes, compliance costs, inflation, growth.

## Transmission layers

1. **Mechanical:** direct supply, demand, cash, collateral, or balance-sheet effect.
2. **Expectation:** revised policy, issuance, inflation, growth, or earnings path.
3. **Portfolio:** duration, currency, credit, commodity, or equity reallocation.
4. **Funding and leverage:** repo, margin, hedging, volatility, and dealer constraints.
5. **Financial conditions:** borrowing cost, credit availability, wealth, and currency.
6. **Real economy:** investment, consumption, trade, fiscal cost, employment, inflation, and growth.
7. **Policy feedback:** central-bank, fiscal, regulatory, or international response.
